# S324 opener — the light-mode coherence bout

**Written**: 2026-07-26, at the close of S323 · **Source**: Captain's screenshot of a real world in light mode (`Screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-26 141043.png`), taken minutes after the S323 sweep shipped.

The frame is unusually good evidence: one window showing four different answers to the same question, three of them mine and one of them a standing design call. S323 fixed the light-mode surfaces that were UNREADABLE. What is left is the surfaces that are readable but **disagree with each other**, which is the harder and more interesting half.

Nothing here is urgent. Nothing here is broken. All of it is visible to Captain in one glance, which is the definition of worth fixing.

---

## 1. Zone labels and pin labels disagree on the same map (MINE, from S323)

**Ground truth.** Both verified in source, both visible in the screenshot.

| | rule | light-mode result |
|---|---|---|
| Pin label | `atlas.css:3230` `background: rgba(var(--atlas-canvas-scrim-rgb), 0.7)` + `color: rgb(var(--atlas-canvas-ink-rgb))` | **dark chip, white text** |
| Zone label | `atlas.css:5831` `background: rgba(var(--atlas-scrim-rgb), 0.7)` + `color: rgba(var(--atlas-ink-rgb), 0.92)` | **white chip, dark text** |

Both are internally consistent and both are legible. They are simply opposite, side by side on the same artwork: "Admiral Splashworth" is a dark chip and "New zone (5)" is a white one, 200px apart.

**This is my own S323 law failing on its own day.** Convention #90 says chrome floating over the user's map artwork holds a dark chip in both themes; I applied it to pin labels and tooltips and did not carry it to zone labels, because zone labels were not in the fleet's finding list and were not broken. That is exactly the "a decision applied to ONE surface is not applied" class, and the sweep across every surface the rule touches IS the call.

**The fix is four characters in two declarations** (swap both to the canvas pair). The work is not the edit, it is the CENSUS: every other thing that draws over the map canvas. Grep candidates to check in the same bout: `.atlas-zone-chip-*`, the zone draft hint, `.atlas-zone-centroid-handle`, marker labels, the map toolbar, the pin trash zone, and the share page's own map render (`.atlas-share-map-*`, which has its own copy of this decision in `tools/show` too). Then own-eyes at both themes on a map with pins AND zones AND markers, which is the state the screenshot happens to show and my S323 walk did not.

**Open question for Captain, worth asking before the sweep**: dark chips over artwork is my call, not his. Light chips might genuinely read better over dark map art. The rule matters more than which way it points — pick one and make all of them obey it.

## 2. The waypoint toast is dark while the toast beside it is light

**Ground truth**: `atlas.css:14082` `.atlas-waypoint-toast { background: rgba(8, 20, 24, 0.92); }` — a literal near-black that no theme touches. The screenshot catches it perfectly: "Places on maps" sits dark with cyan text directly above "Pin placed", which is a normal themed toast and renders light. Two toasts, same corner, same second, opposite treatments.

Not a contrast bug (the cyan is legible on its own dark chip). A COHERENCE bug, and a one-line one once the direction is chosen.

**The call**: waypoint toasts are a celebratory surface with the OW sigil and their own voice, so a deliberately distinct chip is defensible. If it stays dark it should say so with the canvas tokens and a comment, the way the tour root does, rather than a bare literal that reads as an oversight. If it should follow the theme, it is one declaration.

## 3 and 4. The charts room and the home-page chart preview are still black

**This is not a bug; it is a decision from S315, and Captain is now asking whether it still holds.**

The charts plot is a deliberate pinned dark island (`:root[data-theme='light'] .atlas-timeline-view`, and `chart-static.ts` paints its own `#0a0a0a` floor for the home preview and share block). I verified in S323 that the island is intact and its labels read correctly white-on-dark. So today's behaviour is the design working, not drifting.

**The honest cost of changing it, measured rather than guessed.** The chart canvases are drawn in JS, not CSS, so no token flip reaches them:

- `src/visualization/timeline/timeline-view.ts` — **47 colour literals**
- `src/visualization/timeline/chart-static.ts` — **9 colour literals**

Plus the era washes, the overview strip's viewport band, the now-line, the density fan, and the four-family element colours which are themselves baked to their dark column at module load (`element-colors.ts` — the light column exists and is currently unused; the S323 fleet flagged this and the verifier correctly NARROWED it to a documented Phase-0 trade).

So a light charts room is a real bout: a canvas palette module that reads `getTheme()`, ~56 sites re-pointed, the element-colour light column woken, and own-eyes at both themes across chart types 1 and 2, the scroll view, the home preview and a published share block. Call it a session, not an afternoon. Worth doing if light mode is meant to be a first-class theme rather than a preference; worth leaving if the charts reading as a dark instrument panel is the intent.

**A cheaper middle option** if the full palette is not wanted: keep the plot dark and only fix its FRAME, so the dark island looks authored rather than accidental on a light page (a light-mode border, a softer shadow, the card chrome around it in light tokens). That is an hour, not a session, and it addresses the "still black" reading without touching a single canvas literal.
